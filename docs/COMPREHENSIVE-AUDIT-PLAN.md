## Recovery contract and evidence

For each operation record scope/owner, admission, dispatch, validation, commit and publication boundaries; cancellation semantics; timeout/retry/cooldown policy; authoritative state; and reconciliation route. Classify errors as cancelled/superseded, access/input/conflict, recoverable read/provider failure, durable-storage failure, uncertain command, or unexpected defect. A common error envelope may carry a stable code and safe context; the domain remains the recovery owner.

- Preserve last valid data and its freshness; malformed/incomplete input cannot become an empty successful replacement. Preserve draft and accepted user intent separately. Never publish success before required durable work commits.
- Stop ineligible work before dispatch and recheck ownership at acceptance/publication. Cancelling one shared reader must not cancel another eligible reader. Define bounded waiting, queue capacity, attempts and cleanup for every retrying owner; no universal retry wrapper. Preserve provider deadlines across the applicable owner lifetime. MR's two extra page attempts/60-second wait are job-specific, not global defaults.
- Distinguish rejected, admitted, committed, acknowledged and **outcome unknown** mutations. Client abort/timeout is not rollback. Reconcile with authoritative state/revision or a supported idempotency/operation identity. Where proof is unavailable, retain uncertainty and offer an appropriate deliberate next step; never blindly resend an EVE/OBS/local mutation. Do not promise exactly-once external effects.
- Determine local per-entity order/conflict semantics at the authoritative boundary; a browser queue is insufficient proof across tabs/restart. Do not use client timestamps alone as a global intent order. Preserve newer drafts while reconciling an older accepted save.
- Drain owned work before closing dependencies; track worker exit, timers, subscriptions and queued callbacks. Failure to drain is a failed handoff, not success. Storage failure preserves the primary error, prior committed state and actionable recovery; diagnostics must not make it worse.

For defects, retain a minimal regression that fails on the exact pre-fix owner and passes after correction, plus a healthy control. For new logging/toast features, use requirement acceptance tests rather than pretending absence was an old defect. Assert actual persisted state, emitted commands and provider calls where relevant, not just UI text or implementation-shaped internals.

Use deferred promises/barriers and controlled clocks to pause before admission, during wait, after dispatch, before commit, after commit/before acknowledgment, and during drain/restart. Combine selected independent axes: owner change, malformed/truncated/duplicate/out-of-order response, cooldown, cancellation, partial success, storage failure and lost acknowledgment. Include normal success and recovery afterward. Seed any randomized schedules and retain replay inputs. Sleeps alone do not establish an ordering reproduction.

Use small independent oracles for fees/rounding, time windows, accounting and routes; bounded property/metamorphic cases for input permutations, duplicate ingestion, graph constraints, geometry transforms and import/export round trips. Cover zero/negative/unknown values, NaN/infinity, safe integer limits, time units, calendar boundaries/clock jumps, empty/huge/cyclic inputs and tie behavior. Test documented guarantees; do not turn a heuristic into an assumed optimum. Any changed external rule must be verified against a current primary source during execution.

Required interaction chains: Auth→ESI→Core→domain→UI/Stream; commit→event→listener accounting→invalidation; Session transition→travel/combat→report/replay/goals; Maps/feeds→route→itinerary→waypoints; draft→accepted save→lost acknowledgment→reconcile; disable/shutdown/dataset replacement→all admitted work; error→redacted report→optional app toast→user export. Test representative chains and high-risk combinations, not an unbounded Cartesian product. Record fixture failures separately from product evidence.

## Diagnostics and application notification design

Existing owners: `bus.mjs:health()` has bounded listener errors; ESI/Core expose health counters; runtime supervisor uses logger/console; `model.ts:api()` owns private JSON requests without command retries. `index.mjs` owns authenticated APIs, `sse.mjs` bounded delivery, and `dashboard.tsx` the existing private EventSource and single Sonner mount. Settings tabs live in `connections.tsx:Modules()`. These do **not** constitute the requested logging feature today.

Preferred direction: one runtime-owned diagnostic service with a small shared event schema and bounded local sink, integrated with existing lifecycle and authenticated API; browser errors feed the same contract through a bounded private ingress. Use existing dashboard invalidation/SSE ownership for notifications, and read paginated diagnostics only when needed. Keep records separate from durable gameplay/timeline events and public Stream output. Never route diagnostics through the semantic bus in a way that can recursively record its own failure. Do not replace normal error handling or suppress fatal failures to make capture succeed.

Startup/crash errors require a lean supervisor-side adapter outside the child; an unstarted child cannot report itself. P0 must choose the bounded pre-start buffer/sink handoff, one writer/retention owner and explicit crash-tail loss guarantee without importing Core into the supervisor. Avoid competing child/supervisor writers. Disabled capture stops optional writes and clears pending capture buffers; retained reports are governed by the explicit clear policy rather than silently deleted.

Capture schema: bounded event ID, build/runtime epoch, time and monotonic duration where appropriate, severity/code, module/operation, safe correlation/causation ID, owner generation/revision, stage, retry/cooldown decision and known/unknown outcome. Stable safe message templates plus allowlisted context; no arbitrary request/response/error-object serialization. Correlate a browser request with its server operation without making the correlation ID an idempotency guarantee. Detailed mode may add capped stack traces and sampled stage timings; never raw ESI bodies, credentials or user content.

Required controls: **Off / Normal / Detailed**, separately optional error toasts, inspect/filter/copy/export/clear in Settings **Logging**, visible active detail mode and retention/drop counts. Off disables optional capture/storage/buffering and expensive formatting; essential error propagation and existing health/protection behavior continue. Normal records actionable failures and recovery outcomes with safe context. Detailed is deliberate, explained and time-bounded; extension is explicit. Toggling detail off must stop pending expensive capture, not merely hide it.

Before implementation, finalize finite numeric caps for per-event bytes, event rate/burst, duplicate buckets, memory, persistent bytes/age, pending writes, ingress body/rate, UI page size, exports and toast queues. Test each cap and cap+1, repeated unique/duplicate storms and cleanup. Storage/export failure must preserve product work, avoid recursive reports and show a bounded dropped/failed-report indicator. These are proposed starting ceilings for P0 review/measurement, not verified overhead budgets:

| Control | Proposed ceiling / policy |
| --- | --- |
| Normal | 500 records / 2 MiB, 4 KiB per event; classified failures and terminal recovery outcomes |
| Detailed | 2,000 records / 10 MiB, 8 KiB per event; 30-minute opt-in, restore prior Off/Normal mode on expiry/restart; a later explicit user choice takes precedence |
| Admission | 100 pending events / 512 KiB; 10 events/second, burst 50; bounded fingerprint map and reserved terminal-error capacity |
| Retention | At most seven days plus count/byte limits; persistence and crash-tail policy decided at P0 |
| App toasts | Three visible / 20 waiting; per-fingerprint 60-second cooldown; at most three new error toasts per ten seconds, then one aggregate indicator |
| UI/export | 50 records/page; one export at a time, capped at 2,000 records / 10 MiB; show truncation/drop counts |

Backend capture, retention, ingress and export caps apply at the aggregate runtime owner as well as client admission, so multiple tabs cannot multiply them. Toast rendering/action queues are bounded per dashboard; no server coordination of local action callbacks is implied. Settings changes converge across tabs. Clear uses a revision/barrier so delayed uploads cannot resurrect deleted reports; define its interaction with exports and retained reports explicitly. Test detail expiry/restart from both Off and Normal, including a later explicit setting change. Replayed SSE/reconnect history must not repeat old toasts. One logical failure receives one correlation and delivery owner, avoiding duplicate reports/toasts from both `api()` and its caller.

Redact before retaining or transmitting to the private diagnostic endpoint, and recheck server-side/export boundaries. Exclude tokens, auth codes/PKCE, cookies/headers, secrets, raw URLs/query strings, filesystem usernames/paths, notes/mail/chat and private gameplay payloads. Use allowlisted route templates and per-report aliases for sensitive pilot/map/Session identifiers while preserving internal correlation. Seed secrets and private text in tests; assert absence from stored events, UI, copies, exports, supervisor/IPC/fallback-console diagnostic paths and public Stream responses. Diagnostic failure must never fall back to dumping raw errors/context. Export is bounded and user initiated, with a preview/summary of included data. No auto-upload or telemetry permission is assumed.

Application toasts: keep the single Sonner renderer behind one typed publisher/facade for success/info/warning/error/action notices. Inventory direct imports and separate transient banners/notices; migrate publishers in reviewable groups, then guard against new bypasses. Keep contextual form validation, durable job state, confirmation dialogs and guarded undo behavior where they belong. Route transient feedback through the facade without stripping pilot/map/revision guards or duplicating messages. Native desktop/audio notification consent and operational inbox behavior remain intact.

Optional errors use the same facade, stable dedupe keys, per-key cooldown, global rate/capacity limits and overflow summaries. Keep error capture independent of toast enablement. Ensure accessible live-region severity, keyboard dismissal/actions and usable narrow layouts; long-running or actionable notices must have an appropriate inspectable counterpart. Stream Studio save/copy/test-result application feedback is in scope; `server/stream.mjs` toast rules and `public/stream/` delivery are excluded from this migration.

Preserve existing [validation](VALIDATION.md), documented complexity/request bounds and the [historical performance acceptance](https://github.com/BunBajiro/kairo/blob/568ce3e29213e4132a535340c1321e28c46a83cc/analysis/rc17/PERFORMANCE-REVIEW-0.8.0.md). Do not resurrect full-history reads, redundant polling, broad invalidations or per-item provider calls. Each changed owner declares its cost model: requests/attempts/waits, SQL rows/query plans, queues/retained bytes, workers/timers/listeners, render/DOM work and relevant latency. Disabled diagnostics add no diagnostic polling or retained event queue; no logging level increases provider requests.

P0 must fix numerical acceptance budgets **before the corresponding implementation begins** for affected workloads: idle, ordinary use, burst errors, unique-error saturation, large history/map sets, startup and restart. Record central/tail latency, CPU, retained memory/disk and variability; choose tolerances from baseline variance and product requirements. Keep elapsed runs separate from profiling/counters/forced GC. Do not fabricate elapsed claims from request counts or transfer historical percentages to Build97. Budget breaches stay open; material diagnostic overhead requires an explained opt-in, not silent default cost.

## Remaining decisions and limits

1. **Human approval remains required:** durable-data retention/recovery policy and the supported upgrade/full stopped-profile restore boundary. Preserve existing data meanwhile. No automatic backup, portable DPAPI restore, in-place downgrade or general importer is implied by this plan; see [Persistence](PERSISTENCE.md).
2. **Proposed product defaults to settle at P0:** normal local capture enabled, detailed capture and error toasts off; short time-bounded detailed sessions; finite local persistence for restart diagnosis versus memory-only privacy. All need explicit documented caps and actual cost evidence. Bun can revise defaults during plan review; these are not claims of agreement or existing behavior.
3. **Authoritative commands:** inventory which local owners support read-back/revisions/idempotency and decide conflict behavior for cross-tab edits. External EVE/OBS outcomes may remain unknown. Resolve per-owner semantics before implementing retries or claiming cross-process guarantees.
4. **Verification environment:** calibrate budgets and available fixture seams at P0. Native Windows/DPAPI, live permissions/SSO/structures, real OBS output, platform PiP and extended play require separate environment evidence; do not mark these automated-pass or ask Bun to reproduce tractable deterministic defects.

Scope changes, proposed policy trade-offs and evidence gaps belong in the ledger with owner/next action. Completion means all declared automated coverage and required features have reviewed evidence, with residual human/environment boundaries clearly separated. An unresolved confirmed defect, missing required feature acceptance or blocked automated coverage prevents claiming comprehensive completion. A deferral or reduced scope requires Bun's recorded decision; an explicit ledger entry alone does not waive it. Completion still does not mean release authorization.
